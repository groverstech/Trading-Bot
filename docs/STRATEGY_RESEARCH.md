# Strategy research: why the strategies underperform, and what was done about it

Full data tables: [`strategy_report.md`](strategy_report.md), generated by `python -m grokbot.research`.
Numbers below are **out-of-sample (OOS)** results from rolling walk-forward tests on Binance Spot klines for BTC, ETH, SOL, BNB and XRP:

| Timeframe | History | Market conditions covered |
|---|---|---|
| 15m | 1 year | Mostly a declining market |
| 1h | 2 years | Mostly bear: BTC −31%, ETH −44%, SOL −69% |
| 4h | 3 years | Full bull→bear cycle: BTC +69%, BNB +136%, ETH −12% |

Nothing was tuned to make numbers look better. The promotion criteria were written into the code before any results were generated.

## Bottom line

**No strategy qualifies for automated trading.** All five stay available for research but are disabled for testnet and live trading. The system enforces this:
* Testnet and live trading only use strategies that walk-forward validation has promoted.
* Promotion can only be set by `python -m grokbot.research --apply`, never from the UI or API.

## Why the original backtests were misleading

1. **The old backtest did not mirror the live system.** It took every raw signal. The live system also applies:
   * higher-timeframe confirmation;
   * a minimum score;
   * the Sizer's gate, which rejects trades whose net reward:risk after fees and slippage is below 1.5.

   The new simulator applies all three. The gate matters a great deal (signal funnel table):
   * on 15m it removes about 30% of raw signals;
   * on 15m spot, Spot's long-only rule removes another 50%;
   * on 1m/5m almost nothing survives. That is what the live paper session showed in Phase 1.
2. **A real bug:** momentum's `rsi_len` parameter was ignored, because RSI was hard-coded to 14. Fixed. It is now part of the strategy context cache.
3. **Confirmation-timeframe mismatch:** the live scanner used confirmation timeframes even when they were *below* the entry timeframe (e.g. 1h confirming a 4h entry). Live and research now both use only timeframes above the entry timeframe.

## Diagnosis by factor

**Trading fees and slippage.**
* Cost per trade is 0.10–0.13 R on 15m/1h Spot and 0.05–0.07 R on 4h (cost-drag table).
* With 2–3 R targets, the break-even win rate is about 31–34%. The realised OOS win rates sit right at that line, so costs decide the sign.
* Examples of the fee effect:
  * 1h volatility: +75R with no fees, +16R on futures fees, −23R on spot fees.
  * 1h breakout: +40R with no fees, −49R on futures fees.
* Spot's 0.10% taker fee is double the futures fee and is decisive on 1h. Limit (maker) entries were implemented this phase partly for this reason, but the research assumes taker entries, which is conservative.

**Timeframe sensitivity.**
* 15m loses for every strategy, and for momentum, volume, breakout and volatility it loses even with **zero fees**. There is no edge before costs.
* 1h is marginal and fully cost-dependent.
* 4h is the only timeframe where any strategy has a plausible gross edge (breakout, volume and volatility are positive with zero fees).

**Entry conditions.**
* Momentum, volume and breakout enter *after* the move (RSI cross, volume spike, range break). On lower timeframes these signals mean-revert more often than they continue.
* The regime table shows breakout losing −33 to −54R in *range* regimes, and only working in trends on 4h.

**Exit conditions and stop placement.**
* Stops are 1.2–2.5 ATR.
* On 15m/1h, the ATR is small next to round-trip costs, so the stop distance has to be wide relative to the cost, or the net R:R gate rejects the trade.
* There is no trailing stop or time exit. Trades either hit the stop or the target, so win rates are about 30–39%.

**Market regimes.**
* Results flip by regime and direction. For example, trend 1h longs lost 39R while shorts gained 11R during the 2024–26 decline.
* This is directional exposure to the sample's market, not a durable edge.
* A regime filter (e.g. "breakout only when ADX ≥ 25") looks attractive in this table. Adopting it would mean choosing rules on the same data used to evaluate them. It is listed below as a hypothesis to test on fresh data.

**Overfitting.**
* Walk-forward picked different parameter sets in most folds: 2–7 distinct sets per configuration. The optimum is unstable, which is a sign the in-sample best is mostly noise.
* The best in-sample result often fails out of sample. For example, trend 4h: +42R best in hindsight, −31R walk-forward.

**Risk/reward and trade frequency.**
* A higher `rr` raises the net R:R and passes the gate more often, but lowers the hit rate. Neither 2R nor 3R produced a durable edge on 15m/1h.
* Trade frequency is high on 15m: around 1,000 OOS trades per strategy over 5 symbols. That multiplies cost drag.

## What changed in the product

* The walk-forward framework (`grokbot/research/`) with fixed promotion criteria, regime and volatility attribution, cost-drag and parameter-stability reports, and a symbol-concentration check.
* The scanner trades only strategies that are **enabled and promoted**, and only on the timeframes and markets they were validated for.
  * `paper_research_mode` (off by default) lets unpromoted strategies forward-test in **paper only**. It was turned on for this phase's paper session.
* Fixes: `rsi_len`, confirmation-timeframe alignment, and the exact liquidation formula.

## Hypotheses for the next research cycle (not applied)

1. 4h volatility and breakout restricted to trend regimes (ADX ≥ 25), validated on data held out from this study.
2. Maker (limit) entries in the cost model, once paper and testnet fill-rate data show what fraction of limit entries actually fill.
3. Time-based exits and partial take-profits to raise the hit rate.
4. Funding-rate-aware futures direction (avoid paying persistent positive funding on longs).
5. A larger symbol universe, to test whether the 4h volatility result generalises beyond XRP.

Any of these must pass the same, unchanged promotion criteria before it can trade.
